# Awesome Kids

A modern, curated list of high-quality learning resources for kids.

Inspired by the older [`moacirosa/awesome-kids`](https://github.com/moacirosa/awesome-kids), but broadened beyond programming into reading, math, science, creativity, digital safety, and practical life skills.

Last researched: 2026-06-26.

## Selection Criteria

Resources should meet most of these standards:

- Strong learning value, not just screen-time filler.
- Good free tier, low-cost one-time purchase, nonprofit model, school/library access, or unusually strong value for the price.
- Active or still clearly maintained in 2025-2026.
- Age-appropriate design, privacy posture, and parent/teacher controls.
- Clear fit for kids, families, homeschoolers, libraries, or classrooms.
- Minimal ads, dark patterns, social pressure, or noisy monetization.

## Early Learning

- [Khan Academy Kids](https://www.khanacademy.org/kids) - Free, ad-free early learning app for ages 2-8 with reading, writing, math, language, books, videos, creative activities, and teacher tools. Strong first install for preschool through early elementary.
- [PBS KIDS](https://pbskids.org/) - Free games, videos, and apps for young children. Best for families who want low-friction, developmentally appropriate media without a subscription.
- [Pok Pok](https://playpokpok.com/) - Calm, Montessori-inspired digital play for ages 2-8. Good fit when families want open-ended exploration instead of points, levels, and overstimulation. Paid subscription.
- [Toca Boca](https://tocaboca.com/) - Open-ended pretend play apps, especially Toca Boca World. Good for creativity and storytelling; review purchases and in-app store settings before handing it over.
- [Sesame Street](https://www.sesamestreet.org/) - Free videos, games, printables, and family resources around early learning, feelings, routines, and kindness.
- [ABCya](https://www.abcya.com/) - Grade-banded educational games from pre-K through grade 6. Useful for quick practice; adults should watch ads and subscription prompts.

## Reading And Language

- [Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/) - Free, research-based reading information for parents, teachers, and tutors. Best adult resource here for learning how reading develops and how to help when a child struggles.
- [Storyline Online](https://storylineonline.net/) - Free read-aloud videos from the SAG-AFTRA Foundation, with a large library of picture books read by actors and public figures.
- [Epic](https://www.getepic.com/) - Large digital library for kids with books, audiobooks, and learning videos. Strong value for heavy readers; check whether your child's teacher or school provides free classroom access.
- [Sora](https://www.soraapp.com/) - School and library ebook/audiobook access from OverDrive. Excellent value when available through a child's school or public library.
- [Libby](https://www.overdrive.com/apps/libby) - Public-library ebooks and audiobooks. Best for families with library cards and independent readers.
- [Duolingo ABC](https://www.duolingo.com/abc) - Free early reading app focused on letters, phonics, and beginning reading.
- [Start with a Book](https://www.startwithabook.org/) - Reading Rockets sister site with themed booklists and hands-on activities for building background knowledge.
- [Colorin Colorado](https://www.colorincolorado.org/) - Free bilingual literacy guidance for English language learners, families, and educators.

## Math And Logic

- [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/) - Free math practice and lessons from early grades through advanced topics. Best default for structured math support after Khan Academy Kids.
- [Zearn](https://www.zearn.org/) - Nonprofit math curriculum and digital lessons, especially strong for elementary school. Best when used with adult or teacher guidance.
- [NRICH](https://nrich.maths.org/) - Free University of Cambridge problem-solving tasks for ages 3-18. Excellent for kids who need richer thinking than worksheet repetition.
- [Mathigon](https://mathigon.org/) - Free interactive math courses, puzzles, and manipulatives. Especially good for visual learners and middle-grade exploration.
- [DragonBox](https://dragonbox.com/) - Paid math apps that turn number sense, algebra, geometry, multiplication, and chess into game-like learning. Strong value if you prefer one-time app purchases over subscriptions.
- [Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/) - Interactive math, logic, and science courses for older kids and teens. Paid; best for motivated learners who like puzzles and explanations.
- [Polypad](https://polypad.amplify.com/) - Virtual math manipulatives for fractions, geometry, algebra, probability, and patterns. Useful for teachers, homeschoolers, and visual explanations.

## Coding, AI, And Computer Science

- [Scratch](https://scratch.mit.edu/) - Free MIT platform for making games, animations, and stories with block coding. Best general starting point for kids old enough to read.
- [ScratchJr](https://www.scratchjr.org/) - Free introductory coding app for younger kids, usually ages 5-7, using simple blocks and stories.
- [Code.org / CodeAI](https://www.code.org/) - Free K-12 computer science and AI curriculum, including classroom pathways and self-guided activities.
- [Hour of AI](https://hourofai.org/) - Newer global initiative from the Code.org/CSforALL ecosystem for short AI literacy activities. Best for adults who want a guided, current entry point.
- [Tynker](https://www.tynker.com/) - Coding courses, games, Minecraft modding, Python, JavaScript, and AI units. Strong catalog; paid plans can be expensive, so start with free activities first.
- [CodeCombat](https://codecombat.com/) - Game-based Python and JavaScript practice for kids who are ready to type real code.
- [CS Unplugged](https://www.csunplugged.org/) - Free computer science lessons and activities that do not require screens.
- [Blockly Games](https://blockly.games/) - Free browser games for learning programming logic with blocks.
- [CodeHS](https://codehs.com/) - Computer science curriculum and practice for middle school and high school. Good for structured classes and motivated teens.

## Science, Nature, And The World

- [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/) - Free NASA science site with games, crafts, activities, articles, and Spanish-language support.
- [Space Atlas Student](https://saramjh.github.io/space_atlas_student/) - Free-to-access browser models of Moon phases and Earth seasons for adult-guided upper-elementary and middle-school science, with an original Grade 4–8 worksheet. Created by an independent developer; the site uses Google Analytics and AdSense.
- [National Geographic Kids](https://kids.nationalgeographic.com/) - Animals, geography, science, history, quizzes, and short articles with high kid appeal. Watch for Disney/NatGeo privacy and ad settings.
- [Mystery Science](https://mysteryscience.com/) - Standards-aligned elementary science lessons with hands-on activities. Great for teachers and homeschoolers; free trial availability changes.
- [SciShow Kids](https://www.youtube.com/user/scishowkids) - Friendly science videos for younger kids. Use YouTube controls or watch together.
- [The Kid Should See This](https://thekidshouldseethis.com/) - Curated smart videos across science, art, technology, and culture. Better with adult co-viewing than unattended browsing.
- [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/) - Museums, art, history, and culture in explorable formats. Good for older kids and project research.
- [Smithsonian Learning Lab](https://learninglab.si.edu/) - Free Smithsonian collections and activities for teachers, families, and curious older kids.
- [OLogy](https://www.amnh.org/explore/ology) - American Museum of Natural History's science site for kids, covering anthropology, astronomy, biodiversity, Earth science, and more.

## Creativity, Making, And Design

- [Tinkercad](https://www.tinkercad.com/) - Free browser-based 3D design, circuits, and codeblocks from Autodesk. Great bridge from play to making and 3D printing.
- [Canva for Education](https://www.canva.com/education/) - Free for eligible K-12 teachers and students, with templates, visual projects, classroom integrations, and safety controls.
- [Chrome Music Lab](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/) - Free, playful music experiments for rhythm, melody, sound, and composition.
- [Instructables Teachers](https://www.instructables.com/teachers/) - Maker projects and lesson ideas. Quality varies, so adults should pre-screen supplies, tools, and difficulty.
- [Makey Makey](https://makeymakey.com/) - Hands-on invention kit that lets kids turn everyday objects into computer inputs. Paid hardware, but many free project ideas.
- [Sketchbook](https://www.sketchbook.com/) - Drawing app for older kids and teens who want a capable digital sketching tool without heavy complexity.
- [GarageBand](https://www.apple.com/mac/garageband/) - Free Apple music creation app on Mac and iOS. Excellent for audio projects, podcasts, and composition.

## Time, Money, And Life Skills

- [I Can Tell Time](https://icantelltime.com/) - Kids clock learning app for ages 4-8. Free to start, no ads, no tracking, no external links, and a structured path from hour hand basics to reading any analog clock.
- [Practical Money Skills](https://www.practicalmoneyskills.com/) - Free financial literacy games and lesson resources for families and educators.
- [Peter Pig's Money Counter](https://www.practicalmoneyskills.com/en/play/peter-pigs-money-counter.html) - Free coin-counting game for young kids.
- [Money as You Grow](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/money-as-you-grow/) - Free CFPB resource for parents and caregivers with money milestones, activities, conversation starters, and books.
- [KidsGardening](https://kidsgardening.org/) - Gardening activities, lesson plans, and family projects that connect science, responsibility, food, and outdoor time.
- [Red Cross Prepare with Pedro](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/teaching-kids-about-emergency-preparedness/prepare-with-pedro.html) - Emergency preparedness materials for younger kids.

## Digital Safety And Parent Tools

- [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/) - Independent age ratings and reviews for apps, games, movies, books, websites, and shows. Use before adding any high-attention app or game.
- [Be Internet Awesome](https://beinternetawesome.withgoogle.com/en_us/) - Free digital citizenship and safety curriculum, family pledge, and Interland game from Google and safety partners.
- [Google Family Link](https://families.google/familylink/) - Parent controls for compatible Android and Chromebook devices, including app approvals, time limits, web controls, account settings, and location.
- [Apple Screen Time](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105121) - Built-in parental controls for iPhone, iPad, and Mac, including app limits, content restrictions, communication safety, purchases, and web content settings.
- [Microsoft Family Safety](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/family-safety) - Family tools for Windows, Xbox, Microsoft Edge, screen time, activity reporting, and location.
- [YouTube Kids](https://www.youtubekids.com/) - Safer than open YouTube, but not a babysitter. Use approved-content mode for younger kids and review watch history.

## Use With Care

These can be valuable, but they need more adult setup or supervision:

- [Minecraft Education](https://education.minecraft.net/en-us) - Powerful for classrooms, collaboration, coding, history, and STEM. Needs clear goals and account management.
- [Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/) - Can support creativity through Roblox Studio, but social features, monetization, and user-generated content require strong supervision.
- [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/) - Huge educational library, but recommendations and comments are risky for kids. Prefer curated playlists, embedded videos, or co-viewing.
- [Discord](https://discord.com/) - Useful for older teens in structured clubs or classes, but not a kid-safe default.
